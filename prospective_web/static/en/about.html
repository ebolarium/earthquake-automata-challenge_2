PRE-SPECIFIED RESEARCH QUESTION

Can a frozen causal spatial correction improve ETAS across tectonically distinct regions without degrading CSEP calibration?

A 365-day, four-region shadow experiment. Forecasts are locked before each target day, then scored against earthquakes that occur afterward.

Protocol: multi-region-spatial-etas-prospective-v1Baseline: ETASStatus: prospective

One test, four tectonic settings

The same causal architecture is evaluated without regional refitting in California RELM, New Zealand CSEP, the Chilean subduction corridor, and Japan C. Results are reported both by region and as a pre-specified pooled analysis; no region can be removed after activation.

Evidence-gatedPrimary live challengerBlends safe and fixed lines only after prospective evidence accumulates.

Every line preserves the ETAS total expected count for that region-day. The experiment therefore isolates spatial allocation skill rather than winning by forecasting more earthquakes overall.

A causal, one-way evidence gate

Only completed prospective days can affect a later forecast. Fixed-versus-safe event log ratios accumulate in shadow mode, and the next forecast weight is:

w = max(0, tanh((S − log 20) / 2))

The state begins at S=0; retrospective evidence is never imported. Until the BF20 hurdle is crossed, the gated line is identical to the safe line. BF20 is an operational hurdle, not an unconditional 5% false-activation guarantee.

What is measured

Primary: information gain per earthquake

IGPE(M, ETAS) = (1/N) Σᵢ log[λM(i) / λETAS(i)]

Positive IGPE favors the challenger; negative IGPE favors ETAS. Because daily rate mass is preserved, the paired compensator difference is zero.

Known limitations

The method was developed after retrospective outcomes in all four regions were inspected, so those results are not independent confirmation. Catalog completeness and agency continuity differ by region; Japan’s prospective targets use ComCat and are explicitly separated from the historical JMA development catalog. Sparse days can make daily CSEP values volatile, so the final interpretation must include event count, regional coverage, downtime, and uncertainty—not IGPE alone.
